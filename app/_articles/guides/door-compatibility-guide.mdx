## Quick Answer

Most smart deadbolt locks fit a standard North American door with a 1-3/8 inch to 1-3/4 inch thickness, a 2-1/8 inch cross bore, and a 2-3/8 inch or 2-3/4 inch backset. If your door has a mortise lock, multi-point lock, Euro cylinder, thick metal construction, glass near the lock, or an older 1-1/2 inch bore, verify fit before buying.

Measure before choosing a brand. A smart lock can have excellent software and still be wrong for the door.

## Measure These Four Things

| Measurement | Standard fit | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Door thickness | 1-3/8 inch to 1-3/4 inch | Mounting screws and tailpiece must reach correctly |
| Backset | 2-3/8 inch or 2-3/4 inch | Latch must align with the strike plate |
| Cross bore | 2-1/8 inch | Exterior and interior assemblies mount through this hole |
| Lock type | Cylindrical deadbolt | Mortise, rim, and multi-point locks need different products |

## Door Thickness

Open the door and measure from the inside face to the outside face at the lock location. Do not measure a beveled edge or decorative panel.

Standard consumer deadbolt locks usually fit common residential doors. Thick doors may need an extension kit. Thin hollow doors may not support a proper deadbolt. Metal security doors, commercial doors, and ornate doors need more careful checking because trim and reinforcement can interfere with the interior plate.

If your door is outside the lock's published thickness range, do not assume longer screws will solve it. The tailpiece, spindle, and mounting plate also have limits.

## Backset

Backset is the distance from the edge of the door to the center of the deadbolt hole. Most North American doors use 2-3/8 inch or 2-3/4 inch. Many replacement smart deadbolts support both, but you should confirm before ordering.

Wrong backset means the bolt will not line up with the frame. The lock may install but grind, stop short, or fail auto-lock testing.

## Cross Bore and Edge Bore

The cross bore is the large round hole through the face of the door. The common size is 2-1/8 inch. Older doors sometimes have a smaller 1-1/2 inch hole, which may require an adapter plate or careful drilling.

The edge bore is the hole in the door edge for the latch or deadbolt. A standard cylindrical deadbolt usually uses a 1 inch edge bore. If the door has a rectangular pocket instead of a simple round edge bore, you may be looking at a mortise lock.

## Identify the Lock Type

A standard cylindrical deadbolt is the easiest retrofit. It has a separate deadbolt above a knob or lever, a round exterior cylinder, and a thumb turn inside. Most consumer smart locks are designed for this setup.

A mortise lock has a rectangular lock body inside the door edge, often combining latch and deadbolt in one case. A multi-point lock moves bolts at several points along the door. A Euro cylinder uses a long profile cylinder common outside North America. These are not standard deadbolt replacements.

If you have mortise, multi-point, rim, or Euro hardware, shop for that specific lock type or talk to an installer before buying.

## Choose Retrofit or Full Replacement

Retrofit locks keep the exterior hardware and replace only the interior thumb turn. They are useful for apartments, HOA restrictions, historic doors, and renters who cannot change the outside appearance. They still require the existing deadbolt to move smoothly.

Full replacement locks replace exterior and interior hardware. They are better when you want a keypad, fingerprint reader, new finish, or a worn old deadbolt replaced. They require closer fit verification because the exterior assembly must cover the bore and trim area cleanly.

## Special Door Situations

Metal doors can work, but drilling and alignment may require better tools. Glass near the lock can create security and clearance issues. Double doors need a stable inactive leaf before adding a smart deadbolt. Storm doors often have too little clearance between doors for bulky interior assemblies.

For rentals, check lease and exterior appearance rules before installing anything visible outside. Interior-only retrofit locks are often easier to justify, but they still must not damage the door.

## Pre-Purchase Checklist

Before you order, confirm:

1. Door thickness is inside the lock's range.
2. Backset matches the latch supplied with the lock.
3. Cross bore is large enough or can be safely adapted.
4. Lock type matches the product category.
5. Interior assembly has room to clear trim, glass, storm doors, and handles.
6. The existing deadbolt moves smoothly by hand.
7. You have a plan for drilling, strike adjustment, or professional install if needed.

If two or more items are uncertain, do not buy based on product photos alone.

## When to Use a Professional

Use a locksmith or installer for mortise locks, multi-point doors, metal security doors, fire-rated doors, commercial doors, doors with glass close to the lock, or any installation requiring new holes. A poor install can create both security and reliability problems.

For a normal cylindrical deadbolt with standard measurements, DIY installation is usually reasonable if you can align the strike and test the bolt fully.
