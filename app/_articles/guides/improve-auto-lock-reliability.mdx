## Auto-Lock Depends on the Whole Door

Auto-lock is reliable only when the door closes cleanly, the lock knows the correct door state, the delay fits real behavior, and no other system sends conflicting commands. A timer cannot fix a door that bounces open. A routine cannot fix a deadbolt that rubs the strike plate.

Start with the physical door. Close it normally and make sure the latch catches without needing a push. Lock and unlock with the thumb turn while the door is open, then repeat with the door closed. If movement is smooth while open but rough while closed, adjust the strike plate, hinge screws, weatherstripping, or door alignment before changing app settings.

## Make the Door Sensor Trustworthy

If the lock uses a door sensor, the app should report open when the door is open and closed when it is closed. A sensor that always says open may prevent auto-lock. A sensor that says closed while the door is open can throw the bolt into the frame.

Recalibrate the sensor, realign the magnet, and replace any separate sensor battery if the state is wrong. Watch the app while opening and closing the door several times. If the sensor remains unreliable, a simple timer-only auto-lock may be safer until the sensor is fixed.

## Use a Delay People Can Live With

Very short delays create accidental relocks. Thirty seconds may pass before someone brings in groceries, helps a child, or returns to the car for another bag. Start around 5 minutes, then adjust after a week of normal use. If the door is left unlocked too long, reduce it. If people keep getting relocked during entry, increase it.

For better security at night, use a separate bedtime routine or status check rather than making the daytime delay too aggressive. A reliable habit is more valuable than a strict setting that everyone disables.

## Remove Competing Automations

Many smart homes accidentally create two or three auto-lock rules. The lock app may have native auto-lock enabled, HomeKit may have a routine, Alexa may have another, and SmartThings or Home Assistant may also react to the same door sensor. The result can look random because different systems win at different times.

Audit every platform connected to the lock. Search for routines triggered by door open, door closed, lock unlocked, arrival, departure, bedtime, or all doors. Keep one primary rule and disable duplicates. If you rely on a smart-home platform, either turn off native auto-lock or make the native delay longer so it acts as a fallback.

## Prefer Local Behavior for Critical Doors

Some auto-lock runs locally inside the lock. Some depends on a hub, Wi-Fi, cloud service, or automation platform. For exterior doors, native local auto-lock is usually the safest baseline because it can keep working during internet issues. Platform routines are useful as enhancements, but they should not be the only way the door locks itself.

If the lock often appears offline, solve connectivity before blaming auto-lock. A weak hub path or unstable Wi-Fi can delay status updates, drop commands, and make an automation look unreliable even when the lock hardware is fine.

## Test in Real Use

Do more than one clean test. Try keypad unlock, app unlock, thumb-turn unlock, physical key use, and automation-triggered unlock if you use one. Confirm the timer starts when expected and that the bolt extends without grinding. Leave the door open once during testing if a sensor is active, and make sure the lock does not jam itself.

Then watch normal use for several days. If auto-lock only fails during busy moments, the delay is too short or the door is not closing consistently. If failures happen after app or hub commands, look for automation conflicts or connectivity. If the motor tries but cannot finish, return to alignment.

## When to Turn It Off

Disable auto-lock during move-in, maintenance, parties, furniture delivery, or repeated in-and-out work. Re-enable it afterward and confirm the setting took effect.

If auto-lock remains unpredictable after alignment, sensor calibration, battery replacement, and automation cleanup, leave it off until repaired. A manual locking habit plus a nightly status check is safer than an automation that sometimes works and sometimes creates false confidence.
