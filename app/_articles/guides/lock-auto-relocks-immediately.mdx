## Quick Answer

If a smart lock relocks immediately after you unlock it, the auto-lock timer is probably too short, another automation is locking the door, or the door sensor is reporting the wrong state. Increase the lock's auto-lock delay to at least 3 to 5 minutes, disable duplicate routines in smart-home platforms, and test whether the door sensor changes correctly between open and closed.

For a quick temporary fix, turn off auto-lock in the lock app, unlock the door, and confirm it stays unlocked. Then re-enable auto-lock with a longer delay after you find the cause.

## Confirm the Behavior

First identify how fast the relock happens. A relock after 5 to 30 seconds usually points to a short timer or hidden automation. A relock the moment the door closes may point to a door-sensor rule. A relock only when everyone leaves may come from a geofence or away-mode routine.

Test several unlock methods: keypad, app, thumb turn, physical key, and voice assistant if enabled. If only one method causes instant relock, the rule may be tied to that trigger. If every unlock method behaves the same, the lock's native auto-lock setting is more likely.

## Increase the Native Auto-Lock Delay

Open the lock app and look for `Auto-lock`, `Relock`, `Automatic lock`, or `Lock after unlock`. Change the delay to 5 minutes as a baseline. For families, mobility needs, deliveries, or doors used during chores, 7 to 10 minutes may be more realistic.

Avoid 30-second or 60-second settings unless the door is rarely used and people never need time to bring items inside. A smart lock that relocks while someone is still entering will train people to disable the feature entirely.

After changing the delay, test with the door open and then closed. Confirm that the timer starts after the final unlock, not after the first unlock in a sequence.

## Audit Smart-Home Automations

Instant relock often comes from a second system. Check every platform connected to the lock:

| Platform | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Apple Home | Automation tab |
| Alexa | Routines and Hunches |
| Google Home | Automations and Home/Away routines |
| SmartThings | Routines, SmartApps, and lock guest access |
| Home Assistant | Automations, scripts, and blueprints |

Look for triggers such as "when door unlocks," "when door closes," "when everyone leaves," "at night," or "secure house." Disable anything that locks the same door automatically, then test again. Keep one rule, not three.

If you want the smart-home platform to control relocking, turn off the lock's native auto-lock or set it longer than the platform delay so the rules do not compete.

## Check the Door Sensor

Some locks auto-lock when the door sensor reports closed. If the sensor says closed while the door is still open, the lock may relock before you are through the doorway.

Open the app and watch the door status while physically opening and closing the door. If the state is wrong or delayed, recalibrate the sensor, move the magnet closer, replace the sensor battery if applicable, and test again.

If the sensor remains unreliable, disable sensor-based relock and use a timer-only delay until the sensor is fixed.

## Look for Modes and Smart Suggestions

Some ecosystems have modes that can override normal lock settings: away mode, night mode, vacation mode, security routines, or "hunches" that decide the door should be locked. These can feel like random instant relocks because they are not configured inside the lock app.

Temporarily disable these features while testing. Once the door behaves normally, re-enable only the routines you can explain. If a rule exists only because an app suggested it months ago, remove it.

## Use Temporary Overrides

During move-in, maintenance, parties, or repeated trips to the car, turn auto-lock off or use passage mode if your lock supports it. Set a phone reminder to turn it back on. Some locks support schedules, so auto-lock can be off during business hours and on overnight.

If your lock has a physical privacy or passage toggle, learn how it interacts with auto-lock. On some models, privacy mode blocks external access; on others, passage mode keeps the door unlocked. They are not the same setting.

## When the Problem Persists

If the lock still relocks immediately after native settings, automations, and sensor status are cleaned up, reset the lock's auto-lock configuration before doing a full factory reset. Remove only the lock-related routines first.

A factory reset is reasonable only when you cannot identify the rule, the app shows corrupted settings, or the lock was inherited with unknown automations. Document current users and backup access before resetting.
