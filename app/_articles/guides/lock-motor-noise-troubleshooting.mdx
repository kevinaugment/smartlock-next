## Quick Answer

A smart lock motor should make a short, smooth whir and one final click. Grinding, repeated clicking, slow straining, or squealing usually means the deadbolt is rubbing, the batteries are weak, the lock is mounted too tightly, or the internal gears are wearing out. Test the lock with the door open first. If it is quiet with the door open but loud with the door closed, fix door alignment before replacing the lock.

Do not keep forcing a noisy lock. Motor strain turns a small alignment issue into gear wear, battery drain, and eventual failure.

## Start With the Door-Open Test

Open the door and run lock/unlock from the keypad or app. Watch the deadbolt extend and retract. It should move fully without scraping, stalling, or reversing.

If the noise disappears with the door open, the motor is probably fine. The bolt is hitting the strike plate, the door has shifted, the weatherstrip is pushing the door out of position, or the latch is not seating consistently.

If the noise is still present with the door open, look at batteries, mounting tension, lubrication, debris, and internal wear.

## Match the Sound to the Likely Cause

Use the sound pattern as a clue:

| Sound | Likely cause | What to try first |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth short whir | Normal operation | No action |
| Grinding or scraping | Bolt rubbing strike plate | Adjust strike or hinges |
| Slow, labored motor | Weak batteries or high friction | Replace batteries, check alignment |
| Repeated clicking | Gear slip, obstruction, or binding | Test door open, inspect mounting |
| High-pitched squeak | Dry bolt or latch contact | Use graphite or dry silicone where appropriate |
| Buzzing but no movement | Jam, failed motor, or weak power | Stop testing and inspect |

The sound alone is not enough. Always compare door-open and door-closed operation.

## Fix Strike Plate Alignment

The most common cause of loud operation is a bolt that does not enter the strike opening cleanly. The motor has to push against the frame instead of sliding the bolt into open space.

Look for rub marks on the bolt or strike plate. Close the door slowly and watch whether the bolt lines up with the hole. If it is off by a small amount, tighten hinge screws first. If the door has sagged, longer hinge screws into the framing may help. If alignment is close but still rubbing, enlarge or reposition the strike opening carefully.

After adjustment, the deadbolt should extend with the same sound when the door is closed as it does when the door is open.

## Replace Weak Batteries

Weak batteries can make a normal motor sound tired. The lock may still light up and beep, but the motor lacks enough power to overcome small friction. Replace all batteries at the same time with fresh, name-brand cells. Do not mix old and new batteries.

If the noise improves immediately, you found at least part of the problem. If it returns quickly, the lock is working too hard because of alignment, cold weather, high traffic, or internal wear.

## Check Mounting Screws

Overtightened interior screws can compress the lock body and bind the mechanism. This is common after installation or after someone tries to "fix" looseness by tightening everything hard.

Loosen the mounting screws a quarter turn and test again. The lock should remain secure on the door, but the mechanism should not be pinched. If loosening improves the sound, retighten gently until the lock is stable without binding.

## Lubricate the Right Parts

Use dry graphite or a lock-safe dry lubricant on the deadbolt and latch contact points. Work the bolt several times after applying a small amount. Avoid heavy oils inside the lock or keyway because they attract dust and can gum up over time.

Do not spray random lubricant into the motor housing unless the manufacturer recommends it. If the noise is internal gear noise, lubricant may not reach the problem and can create new issues.

## Inspect for Debris and Paint

Fresh paint, wood dust, metal filings, loose screws, and broken plastic can all create scraping or clicking. Remove the interior cover if the manual allows it, inspect visible moving parts, and clear loose debris with compressed air or a soft brush.

If the door was recently painted, confirm the bolt and latch are not dragging through paint buildup. Paint around the strike plate can be enough to make a smart lock sound rough.

## When It Is Internal Wear

If the lock is loud with the door open, has fresh batteries, is not overtightened, and still clicks or skips under no load, the gears or motor may be worn. This is more likely on high-traffic doors, older locks, and locks that spent months fighting a misaligned strike.

Check warranty status before buying parts. Many consumer locks are not economical to repair outside warranty. If the lock is several years old, noisy, unreliable, and draining batteries, replacement may be the better value.

## Prevent Recurrence

Keep the door aligned, replace batteries before they are critically low, and do not ignore new friction. Seasonal movement can make a quiet lock noisy in winter or humid weather. A quick strike adjustment is cheaper than a motor replacement.

For rentals and offices, add motor sound to routine maintenance. If staff report "it still works, just loud," treat that as an early warning, not a cosmetic complaint.
