## Secure Entry Before Troubleshooting Software

If a lock becomes unresponsive after a firmware update, first make sure you can still enter. Keep the door open if you are inside. If you are outside, use the physical key, another working credential, emergency power terminals, or a trusted backup user before changing settings.

Do not repeatedly send app commands while the update state is unclear. If the lock is midway through recovery or reconnecting, repeated commands can drain batteries and make the event log harder to interpret.

## Power Cycle and Wait

Remove all batteries for a full minute. Press a keypad button a few times while the batteries are out, then reinstall fresh batteries in the correct direction. After power returns, wait longer than usual. Some locks rebuild state, retry an update, or reconnect to the hub during the first few minutes.

If nothing lights up, try another known-good battery set and inspect the contacts for corrosion or residue. A firmware update may be blamed when the actual problem is weak cells, one reversed battery, or dirty contacts discovered during the reboot.

## Separate Dead Lock From Offline Lock

Test manual operation first. If the thumb turn or key cannot move the bolt, the issue is mechanical and should be handled like a jam or alignment problem. If manual operation works but the keypad is dead, focus on power, contacts, reset, and warranty support.

If the keypad responds but the app says offline, the lock is not necessarily bricked. It may need Bluetooth proximity, Wi-Fi setup, hub reconnection, or time to report the new firmware version. For Z-Wave, Zigbee, or Matter systems, the hub or controller may also need a refresh before the lock appears normal.

## Let a Stuck Update Resolve Once

If the app still shows an update in progress, give it time. Firmware progress bars are often poor indicators, especially over Bluetooth or sleepy battery radios. If the update has been frozen for a long period, power cycle once and reopen the app near the lock.

Avoid retrying the same update over and over unless the app clearly tells you to. After one clean recovery attempt, gather model, firmware, app version, timestamp, and screenshots for support.

## Reset Only After Recovery Paths Fail

A factory reset can recover a corrupted configuration, but it also removes users, schedules, pairing, automations, fingerprints, and sometimes calibration. Use it only after fresh batteries, a proper power cycle, and reconnection checks fail.

Most resets require the interior program or reset button and a model-specific sequence. Use the current manual for the exact lock. After reset, pair as a new device, set a new master or programming code, re-add only current users, and test manual, keypad, app, and auto-lock behavior.

## Prevent the Next Failed Update

Before updating, install fresh batteries or confirm the lock is comfortably above half. Make sure the physical key or another backup credential works. Keep the phone near the lock if the update uses Bluetooth, and do not start updates right before guests arrive, before leaving town, or during business hours on a critical door.

For hub-based systems, update the hub or controller first when the manufacturer recommends it. A lock update can look broken when the integration layer is stale.
