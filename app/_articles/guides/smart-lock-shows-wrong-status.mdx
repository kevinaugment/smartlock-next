## Check Reality Before Fixing the App

When the app shows the wrong status, start at the door if possible. Look at the bolt, try the handle, and turn the thumb piece. Write down what the app says and what the lock physically does. If the door is actually locked but the app says unlocked, you are likely dealing with sync lag, cache, hub routing, or calibration. If the app says locked but the bolt is not fully extended, treat it as a mechanical problem first.

Remote status is only useful when it matches the door. Until the issue is fixed, verify critical doors manually or through a trusted local person before relying on the app.

## Separate Display Lag From Position Problems

If the lock moves correctly and only the app is stale, force a fresh status check. Pull to refresh if the app supports it, close and reopen the app, stand near Bluetooth locks, and confirm the hub or Wi-Fi connection is online. If another phone shows the right state, the first phone may be caching old data.

If the lock does not fully lock or unlock, fix the mechanism before chasing app sync. Deadbolt drag, a tight strike plate, low batteries, or an overtightened mounting plate can stop the bolt short while the app receives a misleading state.

## Recalibrate After Jams or Battery Changes

Smart locks can lose track of fully locked and fully unlocked positions after jams, battery changes, manual overrides, or repeated partial travel. Look for calibration, door handing, lock orientation, or reset-position settings in the app. Follow the prompts with the door open unless the manufacturer says otherwise.

After calibration, test app status after keypad unlock, app unlock, thumb-turn operation, and auto-lock. If calibration fails or drifts again, inspect alignment. A lock cannot learn accurate positions if the bolt rubs during every cycle.

## Check Door Sensor State Separately

Some systems report both lock state and door state. If the app says the door is open when it is closed, auto-lock and notifications can fail even if the deadbolt state is correct. Realign the magnet, recalibrate the sensor, replace a separate sensor battery, and keep metal interference in mind.

Do not mix these two problems together. A wrong door-open reading is not always a wrong deadbolt reading, and the fix may be in the sensor rather than the lock motor.

## Refresh the Hub or Platform

For Z-Wave, Zigbee, Matter, HomeKit, SmartThings, or Home Assistant setups, the lock app may not be the only source of status. A hub can hold a stale state, miss a report, or route updates poorly. Reboot the hub if every phone sees the same wrong state through that platform. For mesh networks, a repair or heal may improve status reporting.

Manual key or thumb-turn use can also matter. Some locks detect manual position changes better than others. If status is wrong only after someone uses the key, the model may not report manual movement reliably until the next electronic command or refresh.

## When to Escalate

Contact support if calibration does not hold, every user sees repeated wrong status, status errors began after a firmware update, or the app says locked while the bolt is visibly short of full extension. Provide the model, firmware version, hub type, screenshots, and a short video showing the physical lock and app state together.
