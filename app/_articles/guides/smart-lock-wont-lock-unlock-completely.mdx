## Quick Answer

If a smart lock will not lock or unlock completely, test it with the door open. If the deadbolt moves fully while the door is open but stops when the door is closed, the strike plate or door alignment is the problem. If it stops halfway even with the door open, replace batteries, recalibrate the lock, loosen overtightened mounting screws, and inspect for debris or motor wear.

A partially extended deadbolt can look locked while providing poor security. Do not rely on app status until the bolt travels fully and consistently.

## Run the Door-Open Test

Open the door and command lock and unlock from the keypad or app. Watch the bolt. It should extend and retract all the way with a smooth motor sound.

Then repeat with the door closed. If the door-open test succeeds and the door-closed test fails, the lock is hitting the strike plate or fighting door pressure. If both tests fail, the issue is inside the lock, batteries, calibration, or mounting.

This one test prevents unnecessary factory resets and warranty claims.

## Fix Strike Plate and Door Alignment

Most incomplete locking comes from the bolt scraping the strike plate. Look for shiny rub marks on the bolt, strike, or frame. Close the door slowly and see whether the bolt lines up with the strike opening.

Tighten loose hinge screws first. Door sag can move the bolt enough to cause a jam. If alignment is close, carefully enlarge or reposition the strike opening. If weatherstripping pushes the door outward, adjust the strike or latch so the door sits naturally without pressure.

The goal is simple: the bolt should enter the strike with no visible lift, push, scrape, or bounce.

## Replace Batteries

Weak batteries can move the bolt partway and stop under load. Replace all batteries with fresh cells from the same pack, then test again before changing settings.

If new batteries fix the problem only briefly, the lock is still working too hard. Go back to alignment and friction. A good lock should not need fresh batteries to overcome a bad strike plate.

## Recalibrate the Lock

Calibration tells the lock where fully locked and fully unlocked are. A jam, battery change, manual override, or failed cycle can leave the lock confused.

Look for calibration, door handing, reset position, or orientation settings in the app. Run calibration with the door open unless the app instructs otherwise. After calibration, test several full cycles with the door open and then closed.

If calibration repeatedly fails, do not keep retrying. Fix mechanical resistance first.

## Check Mounting Tension

If the problem started right after installation, the interior mounting screws may be too tight or uneven. Overtightening can twist the lock body and bind the mechanism.

Loosen the screws a quarter turn, test the lock, and then tighten gently until the unit is secure without binding. If the lock only works when loose, check door thickness, adapter plates, and whether the tailpiece is installed correctly.

## Inspect for Debris, Paint, and Hardware Changes

Paint buildup, wood dust, metal filings, a shifted faceplate, or a loose screw can stop the bolt. This is common after painting, drilling, door repair, or weatherstrip replacement.

Remove visible debris, clean the bolt path, and avoid heavy oil. Dry graphite or a lock-safe dry lubricant can help the bolt and strike contact points, but lubricant will not fix a misaligned hole.

## Know When It Is Motor Wear

If the bolt moves smoothly by hand, the door is open, batteries are fresh, calibration is complete, screws are not binding, and the motor still stops short, the motor or gears may be failing.

Check warranty status. For a newer lock, contact the manufacturer with a video of the door-open test. For an older or inexpensive lock, replacement may be more practical than motor repair.

## Final Test

After each fix, test ten full cycles: five with the door open and five with the door closed. The bolt should fully extend and retract every time. Then confirm the app status matches the physical state.

For rental or commercial doors, keep a physical key or backup credential available until the lock has passed real-use testing for a few days.
