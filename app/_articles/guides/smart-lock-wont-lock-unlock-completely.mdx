## Use the Door-Open Test First

When a smart lock does not fully lock or unlock, test it with the door open before changing settings. Command lock and unlock from the keypad or app and watch the bolt. It should extend and retract fully with a smooth motor sound. Then repeat with the door closed.

If the lock works with the door open but stops when the door is closed, the strike plate or door alignment is the problem. If it stops halfway even with the door open, look at batteries, calibration, mounting tension, debris, or motor wear.

A partially extended deadbolt can look locked while providing poor security. Do not rely on app status until the bolt travels fully and consistently.

## Fix the Strike Path

Most partial locking comes from the bolt scraping the strike plate or hitting the frame. Look for shiny rub marks on the bolt, strike, or wood. Close the door slowly and see whether the bolt lines up with the opening without lifting, pushing, or bouncing the door.

Tighten loose hinge screws first. If the door has sagged, longer screws into the framing may help. If the alignment is close but still rubbing, adjust or carefully enlarge the strike opening. Weatherstripping can also push the door out of position, especially after seasonal changes.

The goal is boring: the bolt should enter the strike with no visible resistance.

## Rule Out Power, Calibration, and Mounting

Weak batteries can move the bolt partway and stop under load. Replace all cells with fresh batteries from the same pack, then test again before changing settings. If new batteries help only briefly, the lock is still working too hard against friction.

Run calibration, door handing, orientation, or reset-position setup after the mechanism moves freely. Calibration teaches the lock where full lock and unlock positions are, but it cannot learn correctly while the bolt is dragging.

If the issue began after installation, loosen the interior mounting screws a quarter turn. Overtightening can twist the lock body and bind the tailpiece. Retighten gently only after the lock cycles smoothly.

## Check for Debris and Wear

Paint buildup, wood dust, metal filings, a shifted faceplate, or a loose screw can stop the bolt. Clean the bolt path and strike area. Use dry graphite or a lock-safe dry lubricant only on appropriate contact points; heavy oil will not fix a misaligned strike and can attract dirt.

If the bolt moves smoothly by hand, the door is open, batteries are fresh, calibration is complete, and the motor still stops short, the motor or gears may be failing. Record a short video of the door-open test and check warranty status before replacing parts.

## Confirm the Fix Under Real Conditions

After each adjustment, test several full cycles with the door open and several with the door closed. Confirm that the app status matches the physical state. For rental or commercial doors, keep a physical key or backup credential available until the lock has passed normal use for a few days.
