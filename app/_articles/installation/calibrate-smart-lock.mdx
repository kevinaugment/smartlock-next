Calibration teaches the smart lock where fully locked and fully unlocked are. If the lock stops short, reports the wrong status, strains at the end of travel, or starts failing after a battery change, calibration is one of the first fixes to try.

Before calibrating, make sure the door itself is not fighting the lock. A smart lock can only learn reliable positions if the deadbolt moves freely. If the bolt scrapes the strike plate or needs the door to be pushed or lifted, fix alignment first with [Door Alignment Guide](/articles/resources/door-alignment-guide).

## When Calibration Helps

Calibration is useful when the lock status is wrong, the motor stops before the bolt is fully extended, the app shows locked while the bolt is not fully seated, or the lock behaves differently with the door open and closed. It can also help after replacing batteries, reinstalling the lock, changing the strike plate, or correcting door alignment.

Calibration will not fix a dead battery, weak signal, damaged latch, swollen door, or badly misaligned strike. If the thumb turn is hard to operate by hand, solve the mechanical problem first.

## Start With A Manual Check

Open the door and turn the thumb turn from fully unlocked to fully locked several times. The movement should be smooth. Then close the door and repeat. If the closed-door movement is harder, the lock is binding in the strike.

Do not force the motor through a bind. A motor that repeatedly hits resistance can drain batteries, lose position, or report false status. Adjust the strike, tighten hinges, clear paint buildup, or deepen the strike pocket until manual operation feels clean.

## App Calibration

Many modern locks include an app calibration or auto-calibration option. It may be called Calibrate, Auto-Calibrate, Reset Position, Door Handing, or Learn Lock Position.

Start the calibration with the door open unless the manufacturer specifically says otherwise. Follow the prompts while the lock extends and retracts the bolt. Keep the phone near the lock and do not interrupt the process. When the app says calibration is complete, test with the door open and then with the door closed.

If calibration fails, check batteries and alignment before trying again. Low voltage and mechanical resistance are the most common reasons the process cannot learn the endpoints.

## Manual Relearn Method

If the app does not offer calibration, try a simple relearn process. Remove the batteries, wait about 30 seconds, and manually move the deadbolt through its full travel a few times. Reinsert a fresh matched battery set, let the lock boot, then operate it once from the keypad or app.

Some locks relearn their position on the first powered cycle after batteries are restored. Others require a brand-specific programming command. If the lock has a keypad programming menu, check the manual for a calibration, handing, or reset-position function.

## After Calibration

Test the lock several times from different access methods. Use the thumb turn, keypad, and app if available. Confirm the bolt reaches full extension, the app status matches the real door, and the motor sounds normal.

If the lock still stops short only when the door is closed, calibration is not the remaining problem. The strike or door alignment still needs work. If the lock stops short with the door open, inspect the latch, batteries, firmware, and motor behavior.

## When To Reset

Factory reset should be the last step. It may remove user codes, app pairing, automations, and integrations. Use it only after batteries, alignment, firmware, and normal calibration have been checked.

If the lock is used for a rental or business, record any reset and re-test every access method before handing the door back to guests, tenants, or staff. [Test Smart Lock After Installation](/articles/installation/test-smart-lock-after-install) is a good final verification path.

Calibration is a useful maintenance tool, but it works best after the door is mechanically correct. Smooth manual movement first, calibration second, app troubleshooting last.
