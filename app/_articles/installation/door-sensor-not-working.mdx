A door sensor tells the smart lock whether the door is open or closed. When it is wrong, auto-lock can behave badly, the app may show the wrong state, and automations can fire at the wrong time. Most failures come from magnet alignment, gap distance, calibration, or a loose sensor.

Do not rely on auto-lock until the sensor reports accurately. A lock that extends the deadbolt while the door is open can create jams and false security.

## Identify The Sensor Type

Many smart locks use a small magnet on the frame and a sensor in the lock or door. Others use a built-in motion or position sensor. Magnetic sensors are sensitive to distance and alignment, while built-in sensors often need calibration.

Look for a small magnet, adhesive block, or marked sensor point near the lock. If the magnet is missing, loose, painted over, or too far from the sensor, the lock may never see the door as closed.

## Check Alignment And Gap

Close the door slowly and watch where the magnet passes the sensor. The two parts should line up closely when the door is fully closed. If the door has sagged, the frame shifted, or weatherstripping pushes the door outward, the gap may be too large.

Move the magnet closer to the sensor if the manufacturer allows it. Clean the surface before reattaching adhesive parts, and test several open-close cycles before considering the fix complete.

If the door itself is misaligned, fix the hinges or strike area first. The sensor may be telling the truth: the door may not be closing consistently enough.

## Recalibrate The Sensor

Most app-based door sensors have a calibration flow. It may ask you to open and close the door several times while the app learns the positions. Use fresh batteries and keep the phone near the lock during calibration.

Calibrate only after the magnet and door are physically correct. Recalibrating a bad alignment may work briefly, then fail again when the door shifts.

## Clean And Inspect

Dust, paint, moisture, or impact damage can affect sensor behavior. Clean the sensor area gently with a dry or lightly damp cloth. Do not soak the lock or spray cleaner into openings.

Check whether the magnet is cracked, missing, or weak. Replacement magnets are often inexpensive, but polarity and placement can matter, so use the manufacturer replacement when possible.

## Test Automations After Fixing

After the sensor reports correctly, test auto-lock, door-left-open alerts, and any routines that depend on door state. Confirm the app changes from open to closed reliably and quickly.

For broader status problems, see [Smart Lock Shows Wrong Status](/articles/guides/smart-lock-shows-wrong-status). For installation checks, use [Test Smart Lock After Installation](/articles/installation/test-smart-lock-after-install).

## Final Recommendation

Treat door sensor issues as physical alignment problems first and software problems second. Align the magnet, reduce the gap, calibrate, then test automations. If the sensor still fails after that, contact the manufacturer with photos of the magnet position and the app's reported state.
