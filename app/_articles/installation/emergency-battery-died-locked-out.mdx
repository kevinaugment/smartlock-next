If the smart lock battery is dead and you are locked out, stay focused on entry first. Most locks still have at least one emergency path: a physical key, 9V battery terminals, a USB emergency port, another authorized door, or a locksmith.

Do not keep typing codes into a dead keypad. If the lock has no lights or beeps, it needs temporary power or mechanical access before the keypad can help.

## First Check For A Physical Key

If the lock has a keyway, use the backup key. Some smart locks hide the keyway behind a small cover, cap, or rotating trim piece. The physical key works even when the batteries are dead because it operates the lock mechanically.

Once inside, replace the internal batteries with a fresh matched set and test the lock before closing the door again.

## Use A 9V Battery Terminal

Many keypad locks have two small metal contacts on the exterior, often under the keypad or near the bottom of the lock. These contacts are meant for temporary emergency power from a 9V battery.

Buy or find a fresh 9V battery. Hold the battery firmly against the contacts, matching polarity if the lock marks plus and minus. The keypad should light or beep. Keep holding the battery in place while you enter a valid code and unlock the door. After the door opens, replace the internal batteries immediately.

The 9V battery does not recharge the lock. It only gives enough power for temporary keypad operation.

## USB Emergency Power

Some newer locks use USB-C or micro-USB emergency power instead of 9V contacts. Look for a small covered port on the exterior side. Connect a power bank or charger, wait for the lock to wake, then use the keypad or app if supported.

This method depends on the model. If you are unsure, search the lock model and "emergency power" from your phone before forcing any cover open.

## If You Do Not Have The Code

Emergency power only helps if you still know a valid code. If the batteries are dead and you do not have a valid PIN, use the physical key, another entrance, a trusted household member, property manager, or locksmith.

For rentals, contact the host or property manager before attempting anything that could damage the lock or door. For businesses, follow the access escalation process rather than drilling the lock without authorization.

## When To Call A Locksmith

Call a locksmith when there is no key, no emergency power method, no reachable authorized user, and no safe alternate entrance. Ask for non-destructive entry first. Drilling should be the last resort because it can destroy the lock and may damage the door hardware.

If the lock is on a rental, office, or managed property, document the lockout and the locksmith work. That record helps later if batteries, maintenance policy, or backup access need to be improved.

## Prevent It Next Time

Replace smart lock batteries before the final warning. For busy homes, rentals, and offices, replace earlier than you would for a low-risk interior door. Keep a physical key or backup access method available, and make sure the people who may need entry know the process.

Use [How to Change Smart Lock Battery](/articles/installation/how-to-change-smart-lock-battery) for the replacement procedure and [Smart Lock Battery Life Guide](/articles/installation/smart-lock-battery-life-guide) for longer-term prevention.

## Final Recovery Check

After entry, replace batteries, test the keypad, test the app, confirm the lock reports online, and verify the deadbolt moves smoothly. If fresh batteries die quickly again, investigate door alignment, signal quality, or a failing lock rather than treating it as a one-time event.
