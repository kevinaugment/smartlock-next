Changing smart lock batteries is usually simple, but doing it carelessly can create false low-battery warnings, offline status, weak motor behavior, or a lock that will not power on. Use a fresh matched set, install them in the correct direction, and test the lock before walking away.

Do not wait until the lock is nearly dead. Replace batteries when the app warns you, when the keypad shows a low-battery signal, or before a busy rental or travel period. For managed properties, set a replacement schedule instead of hoping someone notices the final warning.

For battery life planning across protocols and climates, see [Smart Lock Battery Life Guide](/articles/installation/smart-lock-battery-life-guide).

## When To Replace

Replace immediately if the app reports low battery, the keypad flashes a low-battery warning, the lock responds slowly, the motor sounds weak, or the lock repeatedly goes offline. A weak battery can still light the keypad while failing to move the deadbolt reliably.

As a conservative policy, replace around 30 percent for rentals, offices, and doors where lockout risk is expensive. Homes can sometimes wait longer, but there is little benefit to squeezing the last few days out of batteries on an exterior door.

Wi-Fi locks often need more frequent replacement than Zigbee, Z-Wave, or Thread locks. Heavy use, cold weather, poor signal, and door misalignment can shorten the interval for any protocol.

## Choose The Right Batteries

Use the battery type recommended by the manufacturer. Most smart locks use four AA batteries, but some models use different formats. Check the manual or the label inside the battery compartment before buying replacements.

Use a matched set from the same brand, chemistry, and package. Do not mix old and new cells. Do not mix alkaline and lithium cells. Avoid bargain batteries for exterior doors or rental properties where a failed set creates a support problem.

Alkaline AA batteries are fine for many indoor locks and mild climates. Lithium AA batteries cost more but perform better in cold weather and can be the better choice for exposed exterior doors. Rechargeable batteries may work in some locks, but their voltage can confuse battery reporting unless the manufacturer supports them.

## Replacement Steps

Open the interior battery cover. Some covers slide off; others use small screws. Take a photo before removing the old batteries if the polarity markings are hard to see.

Remove all old batteries and inspect the compartment. If you see corrosion, moisture, or debris, clean the contacts gently with a dry cloth. Do not scrape aggressively or leave liquid inside the compartment.

Insert the new batteries in the exact polarity shown by the lock. Make sure every cell is seated firmly against the contacts. Replace the cover, then give the lock a few minutes to initialize and reconnect to the app or hub.

## Test After Replacement

Test the lock locally first. Use the thumb turn, then the keypad code. If the motor sounds normal and the keypad responds correctly, test app control and confirm the lock reports online.

Battery percentage may not update instantly. Some locks need time to estimate the new battery level. If the app still shows an old reading after a day, restart the app, wake the lock, or check the manufacturer's calibration guidance.

If the lock does not power on, remove the batteries and check polarity. If it powers on but stays offline, wait a few minutes, wake the keypad, then restart the hub or router if needed. Do not factory reset the lock just because the first reconnect is slow.

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is mixing batteries. One weak cell can make the whole set unreliable. The second is inserting one battery backward. The third is replacing batteries while ignoring a rubbing deadbolt that caused the drain in the first place.

If new batteries drain quickly, check door alignment and signal quality. A lock that struggles to throw the bolt or reconnects constantly will burn through fresh batteries. See [Door Alignment Guide](/articles/resources/door-alignment-guide) and [Improve Connection Stability](/articles/protocols/improve-connection-stability).

## Disposal

Recycle used batteries according to local rules. Many hardware stores, electronics stores, and municipal programs accept household batteries. Do not leave old batteries inside the lock compartment or mix them back into your fresh supply.

## Final Recommendation

Keep one fresh matched set available, replace before the lock becomes unreliable, and test every access method after replacement. Battery changes should be routine maintenance, not an emergency recovery process.
