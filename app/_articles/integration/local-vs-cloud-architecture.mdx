The local-versus-cloud decision determines what your smart lock depends on. A cloud lock may be easier to set up and manage remotely, but it depends more heavily on the internet and vendor services. A local hub system takes more planning, but it can keep core access working when the internet is down.

This is not a purity contest. Many good systems are hybrid. The right architecture depends on who needs access, how bad an outage would be, how much privacy matters, and whether someone can maintain a hub.

## Local Control

Local control means the lock can receive commands through a hub, controller, or border router inside the property. Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread, HomeKit, Hubitat, Home Assistant, and some SmartThings setups can provide local behavior depending on the exact device and automation.

The advantage is resilience. If the internet is down but the local hub and network still have power, basic lock control and automations may continue. Local systems can also feel faster because commands do not always need to travel through a vendor cloud.

The tradeoff is setup and maintenance. You may need a hub, repeaters, firmware updates, backups, and more deliberate troubleshooting. A poorly placed hub can make a local system unreliable even if the architecture is strong.

## Cloud Control

Cloud control means commands and account logic depend on vendor servers. Wi-Fi locks often use this model because it makes remote access simple: the lock connects to the router, the app talks to the vendor cloud, and the vendor routes commands back to the lock.

The advantage is convenience. Setup is often simpler, remote access is built in, and the vendor can add features without requiring the user to manage a local controller.

The tradeoff is dependency. Internet outages, cloud outages, account issues, app problems, or a discontinued service can reduce smart features. Local keypad access and physical keys may still work, but remote control and automations may fail.

## Hybrid Systems

Hybrid systems combine local and cloud behavior. Basic lock and unlock may run locally, while remote access, logs, voice assistants, guest codes, or integrations use cloud services. This is often the most practical model when configured well.

The key is to confirm which features stay local. "Works with Matter" or "supports HomeKit" does not automatically mean every feature is local. Guest-code management, audit logs, or advanced automations may still depend on a vendor app.

## Comparison

| Decision factor | Local control | Cloud control |
|---|---|---|
| Internet outage | Core functions may continue | Smart features often stop |
| Setup effort | Higher | Lower |
| Remote access | Requires hub or remote bridge | Usually built in |
| Latency | Often faster on site | Depends on cloud round trip |
| Privacy | More data can stay local | More data may pass through vendor services |
| Long-term risk | Depends on your hub/controller | Depends on vendor service continuity |

For rentals and commercial doors, the outage behavior matters more than the app screenshot. If a guest, tenant, employee, or cleaner needs access during an outage, verify offline keypad behavior and backup entry before relying on the lock.

## Privacy And Logs

Cloud systems can expose more access metadata to the vendor: when the door was used, which account created a code, which phone issued a command, and when remote access occurred. That may be acceptable for a home and more sensitive for workplaces, rentals, healthcare, or tenant settings.

Local systems can reduce exposure, but they still need privacy discipline. Logs stored locally are still personal data when tied to people. Review [Data Privacy Compliance](/articles/security/data-privacy-compliance-guide) and [Audit Trail Forensic Analysis](/articles/security/audit-trail-forensic-analysis) if logs will be used formally.

## How To Choose

Choose a cloud-first lock for a simple single-door home where convenience matters and keypad or key backup is enough during outages. Choose local or hybrid control when access must continue during internet loss, when privacy matters, or when you manage multiple doors.

For Airbnb, rentals, offices, and regulated sites, do not buy until you have tested what works without internet. The best architecture is the one that keeps the right people able to enter while limiting unnecessary dependency and data exposure.
