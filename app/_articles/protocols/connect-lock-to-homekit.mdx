Adding a smart lock to Apple Home is usually straightforward if the lock supports HomeKit or Matter and the home has a working Home Hub. Most setup failures come from missing pairing codes, weak Thread or Bluetooth range, old firmware, or trying to add the lock before it has been installed and tested.

Before pairing, confirm which Apple path the lock uses. A Works with Apple Home lock uses HomeKit. A Matter lock can be added to Apple Home and may also be shared with other ecosystems. A Thread lock needs a Thread border router such as a HomePod mini, HomePod, or Apple TV 4K.

Use [Best HomeKit Smart Locks](/best/homekit-smart-locks) and [Best Matter Smart Locks](/best/matter-smart-locks) if you are still choosing hardware.

## Before Pairing

Install the lock, insert fresh batteries, and test keypad or local operation before opening the Home app. Pairing a lock that has not been mechanically tested makes troubleshooting harder because you cannot tell whether the issue is HomeKit, firmware, battery, or door alignment.

Find the HomeKit or Matter setup code. It may be on a card in the box, inside the battery cover, on the lock body, or in the manufacturer's app. Keep a secure record of the code after setup; losing it can make future pairing harder.

Make sure your iPhone is updated and close to the lock. If the lock uses Thread, confirm the Home Hub is online. If it uses Bluetooth during setup, stand near the door.

## Pairing In Apple Home

Open the Home app, tap Add Accessory, and scan the HomeKit or Matter code. Assign the lock to the correct home and room, then give it a clear name such as Front Door or Garage Entry. Avoid naming multiple devices simply "Lock."

After pairing, test lock and unlock from the Home app. Then manually operate the lock and confirm Apple Home updates the status correctly. If status does not update, wait briefly, wake the lock, and check the Home Hub.

For a broader setup workflow, see [Smart Lock Pairing Guide](/articles/guides/smart-lock-pairing-complete-guide).

## Fix Unable To Add Accessory

If Apple Home cannot add the lock, start with the basics: move the iPhone closer, wake the lock, confirm batteries are strong, and check that the lock is not already paired to another home or controller.

For Matter devices, some locks need to be reset or put back into commissioning mode before they can be added again. For HomeKit devices, an old HomeKit pairing may need to be removed from the manufacturer's app or reset through the lock's setup process.

Restart the iPhone and Home Hub if pairing repeatedly fails. Update the lock firmware through the manufacturer app if the lock can be reached there.

## Fix No Response

"No Response" usually means Apple Home cannot reach the lock through the expected path. For Bluetooth locks, the Home Hub may be too far away. For Thread locks, the Thread mesh or border router may be weak. For Wi-Fi or bridge-based locks, the router or vendor bridge may be offline.

Check whether the lock works from the manufacturer's app. If it works there but not in Apple Home, focus on Home Hub status, Apple Home permissions, and HomeKit/Matter pairing. If it fails everywhere, check batteries, signal, and the lock's own connectivity.

Use [Improve Connection Stability](/articles/protocols/improve-connection-stability) if the lock often drops offline.

## Guest And Rental Limits

Apple Home is excellent for personal and household control, but it is not always the best guest-code system for Airbnb or tenant access. Guests should not need to join your Apple Home just to enter a rental. For those cases, choose a keypad lock with scheduled PINs and use HomeKit mainly for owner status, alerts, and automation.

For Apple-specific buying tradeoffs, read [Apple Home Key Smart Locks Guide](/articles/protocols/apple-home-key-smart-locks-guide).

## Final Check

After setup, test app control, keypad access, physical key or backup access, notifications, and any auto-lock automation. Apple Home should be part of a reliable access plan, not the only way into the door.
