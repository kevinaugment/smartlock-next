## Quick Answer

Enterprise smart lock protocol selection starts with operations, not radios. Door count, credential lifecycle, audit logs, identity integration, network capacity, fire code, and support ownership should decide whether Wi-Fi, Z-Wave, Matter/Thread, wired access control, or a managed platform is appropriate.

Use the [Fleet Planner](/calculators/fleet-planner) and [Access Capacity Calculator](/calculators/access-capacity) before shortlisting hardware.

## Enterprise Protocol Decision Table

| Deployment need | Better starting point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One small office | Wi-Fi or managed cloud lock | Fast setup and simple admin |
| Thick-wall commercial building | Z-Wave or wired access control | Better physical reliability planning |
| Multi-site portfolio | Managed platform | Central support, monitoring, and reporting |
| Apple/consumer amenity space | Matter/HomeKit pilot | Good resident or staff UX, but verify controls |
| High-security doors | Wired access control | Egress, audit, and monitoring requirements |
| Offline-critical access | Local credential storage | Reduces internet and cloud outage risk |

## What Matters More Than Protocol

For enterprise teams, protocol is only one layer. The deployment also needs:

- Named user lifecycle and deprovisioning.
- Staff, vendor, visitor, and emergency roles.
- Audit-log retention and export.
- HR, badge, visitor, or PMS integration.
- Battery or power maintenance policy.
- Help desk playbooks for lockouts and failed credentials.
- Privacy and access-log governance.

## Network and Power Planning

Wi-Fi locks add router load, cloud dependency, and battery maintenance. Z-Wave and Zigbee add hub and repeater design. Matter/Thread adds controller and border-router planning. Wired doors add PoE, voltage drop, fire egress, and installer coordination.

Use the [Network Bandwidth Calculator](/calculators/network-bandwidth) and [PoE Power Budget Calculator](/calculators/poe-power) when the deployment touches commercial infrastructure.

## Pilot Before Standardizing

Pilot across different door types: exterior doors, fire-rated doors, elevator-adjacent corridors, metal frames, high-traffic staff doors, and remote sites. Measure command success, battery drain, credential sync, support tickets, and audit-log usefulness.

Do not treat a successful single-door demo as proof that a 300-door rollout is ready.

## Compliance and Security

Commercial doors may be subject to fire, ADA, insurance, privacy, and security requirements. Consumer lock convenience should not override egress, emergency access, or identity-management controls.
