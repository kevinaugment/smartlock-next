Smart lock stability usually comes down to signal quality, power, and route design. If a lock is far from the router, hub, repeater, or Thread border router, it may go offline, respond slowly, or drain batteries through repeated retries.

The best fix is often boring infrastructure: move the hub, add a powered repeater, improve Wi-Fi coverage, or reduce interference. Changing apps rarely fixes a weak radio path at the door.

## Identify The Connection Type

Start by confirming how the lock talks to the system. A Wi-Fi lock connects to the router. A Zigbee or Z-Wave lock connects through a hub and mesh. A Thread lock needs a Thread border router. A Bluetooth lock may only be reliable when a phone or bridge is nearby.

This matters because each system fails differently. Wi-Fi locks suffer from weak 2.4 GHz coverage and router changes. Zigbee and Thread can suffer from 2.4 GHz interference and sparse mesh routing. Z-Wave often handles walls better, but still needs repeaters for distance and difficult layouts.

For protocol tradeoffs, compare [Wi-Fi vs Zigbee vs Z-Wave](/articles/protocols/smart-lock-protocols-overview).

## Check Signal At The Door

Measure or inspect signal where the lock is installed, not from the couch or next to the router. Exterior doors often sit near metal frames, brick, glass, concrete, stairwells, and appliances. These can weaken signal even in a home that otherwise has good coverage.

Many apps show signal strength, RSSI, LQI, or connection quality. If the number is poor or the app only reports a vague weak signal, treat that as a real installation issue. Use the [Signal Strength Calculator](/calculators/signal-strength) or [RF Coverage Estimator](/calculators/rf-coverage) to plan improvements.

## Add The Right Repeater

For Zigbee, Z-Wave, and Thread, add a powered device between the hub and lock. Smart plugs, dedicated range extenders, switches, and powered routers can help. Battery sensors usually do not repeat traffic, so they should not be counted as infrastructure.

Place the repeater halfway between the hub and the lock, then give the mesh time to rebuild. Some systems update routes automatically after a few minutes; others may need a network repair or a device wake-up.

For Wi-Fi locks, improve 2.4 GHz coverage near the door. A mesh Wi-Fi node or access point is usually better than a cheap extender if the lock is important.

## Reduce Interference

Keep hubs and routers away from metal cabinets, large appliances, concrete corners, and crowded power strips. For Zigbee and Thread, avoid placing the hub directly beside a Wi-Fi router if interference is likely. Adjusting Wi-Fi channels can help when many 2.4 GHz networks overlap.

Interference often appears as time-of-day instability. If the lock works in the morning but struggles in the evening, neighboring Wi-Fi, household streaming, or device traffic may be contributing.

## Check Batteries And Door Alignment

Weak batteries can make a lock look like it has a network problem. The radio may reconnect poorly, and the motor may take longer to finish commands. Replace batteries with a fresh matched set before deeper troubleshooting.

Door alignment also matters. A motor that fights the strike plate stays active longer and may time out before reporting status. If the lock is slow only when the door is closed, fix the strike or deadbolt alignment before changing network settings.

See [Smart Lock Battery Life](/articles/installation/smart-lock-battery-life-guide) and [Door Alignment Guide](/articles/resources/door-alignment-guide) for those checks.

## Update And Monitor

Update the hub, app, and lock firmware after the physical and signal issues are addressed. Firmware can improve routing and reconnect behavior, but it is not a substitute for coverage.

After changes, test several lock and unlock commands, then monitor for a few days. Track whether the lock goes offline, whether commands time out, and whether battery drain changes. If the problem returns after power outages, read [Smart Lock Disconnects After Power Outage](/articles/protocols/smart-lock-disconnects-after-power-outage).

## Final Recommendation

Improve the path before replacing the lock. A stable smart lock needs strong signal at the door, a healthy hub or router, fresh batteries, smooth deadbolt movement, and a backup access plan. When those basics are right, most connection problems disappear.
