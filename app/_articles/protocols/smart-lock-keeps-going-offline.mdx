If a smart lock keeps going offline, start with signal and power. Most repeated offline problems come from a weak radio path, sparse mesh, poor router or hub placement, low batteries, or a door that makes the motor work too hard.

Do not factory reset the lock first. A reset may remove users and automations while leaving the real cause untouched.

## Confirm What Still Works

Test local access before network troubleshooting. If the keypad and thumb turn work, the lock is not dead; it is losing communication. If local access is slow or unreliable, replace batteries and check alignment before focusing on the hub.

Also check whether other smart-home devices are offline. If many devices dropped at once, the hub, router, internet, or platform is the likely issue. If only one lock drops, focus on that door's signal, batteries, and installation.

## Check Signal At The Door

Look for signal strength, RSSI, LQI, or connection quality in the app or hub. If the app does not expose a number, use behavior as evidence: daily offline events usually mean poor signal, while one or two events a month may be marginal coverage.

Exterior doors are difficult locations. Metal frames, brick, concrete, appliances, stairwells, and distance can weaken signal even when the rest of the home has good coverage.

## Fix The Network Path

For Wi-Fi locks, improve 2.4 GHz coverage at the door. Move the router, add a better access point, or reduce interference. For Zigbee, Z-Wave, or Thread locks, add a powered repeater between the hub and the lock. Battery sensors usually do not repeat traffic.

After adding repeaters or moving equipment, give the network time to rebuild routes. Wake the lock from the keypad and test several app commands.

Use [Improve Connection Stability](/articles/protocols/improve-connection-stability) for the broader optimization path.

## Check Batteries And Alignment

Weak batteries can cause radio problems before the lock looks fully dead. Replace all batteries with a fresh matched set. Do not mix old and new cells.

Then test the deadbolt with the door open and closed. If the bolt moves smoothly when open but struggles when closed, the motor may be timing out or draining batteries because of alignment. Fix the strike before replacing the lock.

## Power Outage Pattern

If the lock only goes offline after outages, the problem may be restart order. Routers, hubs, repeaters, and border routers need time to come back before a battery lock can reconnect. A small UPS for the router and hub can prevent many of these events.

Read [Smart Lock Disconnects After Power Outage](/articles/protocols/smart-lock-disconnects-after-power-outage) for that recovery path.

## When To Replace Hardware

Consider hardware replacement only after fresh batteries, improved signal, firmware updates, and alignment checks. If a lock goes offline even within a few feet of the hub or router, the radio or internal electronics may be failing.

The practical fix order is simple: batteries, local operation, signal path, repeaters, firmware, then hardware support.
