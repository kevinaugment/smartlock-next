## Wi-Fi Drain Is Usually a System Problem

Wi-Fi locks use more power than sleeping mesh locks, but fast battery drain is rarely just "Wi-Fi is bad." The usual causes are weak 2.4 GHz signal, repeated cloud retries, motor drag, cold weather, heavy guest use, firmware behavior, or integrations that wake the lock too often.

Estimate the expected replacement window with the [Battery Life Calculator](/calculators/battery-life), then check link quality at the door with the [Signal Strength Calculator](/calculators/signal-strength). Testing beside the router does not tell you what the lock experiences.

## Fix Door Load Before Network Tuning

A deadbolt that rubs the strike plate makes every lock cycle expensive. Test the lock with the door open and closed. If it is smooth open but strained closed, fix alignment, hinge sag, weatherstripping, or strike depth before blaming batteries.

Replace unknown batteries with fresh cells from the same pack and avoid mixing old and new batteries. In cold weather, alkaline cells lose usable capacity sooner, so replacement thresholds should be higher for exterior doors.

## Improve Signal and Reduce Wakeups

Weak Wi-Fi causes retries. Move the router or add an access point so the 2.4 GHz signal at the door is stable. Reduce unnecessary automations, dashboards, or integrations that poll status constantly. Firmware updates can help, but read model-specific notes first and update only when you can test the lock afterward.

For rentals, count operations. Guest arrivals, cleaners, maintenance, and repeated check-ins can turn a normal battery estimate into a short maintenance cycle. Replace before high-occupancy periods rather than waiting for a low-battery alert.

## When to Switch Protocols

Switch away from direct Wi-Fi when batteries routinely fail before the maintenance window, signal cannot be improved, there are many doors, or emergency lockouts create expensive labor. Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread, or bridge-based designs can reduce radio drain, but they introduce hub and coverage planning.

Use the [Protocol Selection Wizard](/calculators/protocol-wizard) before changing hardware. A mesh protocol with poor repeater placement can be just as frustrating as a Wi-Fi lock with weak signal.
