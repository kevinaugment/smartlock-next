## Quick Answer

Choose Wi-Fi when you need the simplest one-door remote setup and can tolerate shorter battery life. Choose Z-Wave when range, wall penetration, hub-based automation, and repeatable multi-door maintenance matter more than no-hub convenience.

For apartments, rentals, and larger homes, test the route from hub to lock with the [Signal Strength Calculator](/calculators/signal-strength) before buying repeaters.

## Wi-Fi vs Z-Wave Comparison

| Decision area | Wi-Fi smart lock | Z-Wave smart lock |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Usually easiest | Requires Z-Wave hub |
| Range basis | Router and 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi quality | Sub-GHz mesh and repeaters |
| Battery life | Often shorter | Usually longer |
| Remote access | Often built into brand app | Hub or platform dependent |
| Best fit | One or two doors | Rentals, thick walls, portfolios |
| Failure mode | Weak Wi-Fi, cloud delays, battery drain | Bad hub placement or missing repeaters |

## Range and Wall Penetration

Wi-Fi is convenient, but exterior doors are often far from the router and surrounded by dense materials. Z-Wave's sub-GHz radio can be more forgiving through walls and corridors, especially in apartments and older buildings.

This does not mean Z-Wave is automatic. You still need a compatible hub, secure inclusion, and powered repeaters where the signal path is weak.

## Battery Replacement Burden

Wi-Fi locks can work well for homeowners who can replace batteries quickly. The same lock can become expensive in rentals when each low-battery event creates a guest message, cleaner visit, or emergency call.

Z-Wave usually reduces radio drain, but battery life still depends on door alignment, operation count, temperature, and firmware.

## Rental Operations

| Rental scenario | Better starting point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One local Airbnb | Wi-Fi | Fast setup and direct app control |
| Remote vacation rental | Z-Wave | Better battery and offline planning |
| Long-term rental | Depends on hub ownership | Tenant router changes can break Wi-Fi workflows |
| Multifamily corridor | Z-Wave | Sub-GHz range and repeaters are easier to standardize |
| Mixed consumer property | Wi-Fi or Matter | Simpler resident setup may matter more |

## Enterprise Caveat

Z-Wave can help with lock-first reliability, but enterprise access control still needs audit logs, credential lifecycle, identity integration, privacy rules, and support workflows. Protocol alone does not make a consumer lock enterprise-ready.
