## Executive Summary

The **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)** applies to all public accommodations and commercial facilities (offices, hotels, retail). It also applies to "adaptable" units in multi-family housing.

For smart locks, ADA compliance isn't just about "can I reach it?". It is about **Operational Force** and **Dexterity**.

**The Golden Rule**: "Operable limits utilizing one hand and not requiring tight grasping, pinching, or twisting of the wrist."

---

## Core Requirements (ADA Standards 404.2.7)

### 1. No Grasping or Twisting
*   **Round Knobs are Banned**: You cannot operate a round knob if you have arthritis or limited hand function.
*   **Required**: **Lever Handles** that can be depressed with an elbow or closed fist.
*   **Smart Lock Impact**: Many smart locks are "Deadbolts Only". You must pair them with an ADA Passage Lever below.

### 2. Operational Force
*   **Limit**: Maximum **5 lbs (22.2 N)** of force to retract the latch.
*   **Smart Lock Impact**: Some motorized locks have stiff gearing when operated manually. If turning the thumbturn takes 10 lbs of torque, it fails ADA.

### 3. Mounting Height
*   **Code**: Hardware must be mounted between **34 inches** and **48 inches** above the finished floor.
*   **Smart Lock Impact**: Keypads cannot be mounted at eye level (60"). They must be reachable from a wheelchair.

## The "Smart" Advantage for Accessibility

While physical locks have hurdles, Smart Locks actually **Solve** many ADA problems:

1.  **Proximity Unlock**: A wheelchair user does not need to lean forward to insert a key. An auto-unlock (Bluetooth) feature is a massive accessibility win.
2.  **Voice Control**: "Alexa, unlock the front door" allows operation with zero physical contact.
3.  **Remote Entry**: Users with limited mobility can unlock the door for a caregiver without getting up.

## Compliance Checklist for Installers

Use this before signing off on a commercial job:

| Check | Requirement | Pass/Fail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Handle Shape** | Is it a lever (or push bar)? NOT a knob or thumb-latch? | [ ] |
| **Thumbturn** | Is the interior thumbturn a "Blade" shape? (Not a round disc)? | [ ] |
| **Clearance** | Is there 18" of clear wall space on the pull side of the door? | [ ] |
| **Touchscreen** | Does the touchscreen work with a prosthetic (Capacitive vs Resistive)? | [ ] |
| **Feedback** | Does the lock give Audio AND Visual feedback (Beep + Flash)? | [ ] |

### The "Blade" Thumbturn Rule
Many consumer smart locks (like early August models) had huge, round, slippery aluminum knobs for the interior turn. These are **not ADA compliant**.
*   **Fix**: Manufacturers now sell "Wing" adapters that slip over the round knob to create a lever, allowing operation with the side of a hand.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does ADA apply to my house?
Generally, no. Private single-family homes are exempt. However, if you run a business from home (client visits) or rent it out (Airbnb), you may have obligations. Also, "Aging in Place" design suggests following ADA even if not required, for your own future benefit.

### Can I get a tax credit for upgrading?
Yes. Businesses can often claim the **Disabled Access Credit** (IRS Form 8826) for removing barriers, which can cover 50% of the cost of installing accessible smart locks (up to $5,000).

### Are capacitive touchpads ADA compliant?
This is a gray area. Some argue they require "skin contact" which fails for some prosthetics. Best practice commercial keypads use physical rubber/metal buttons that provide tactile feedback.
