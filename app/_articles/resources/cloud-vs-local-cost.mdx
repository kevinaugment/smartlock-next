Cloud and local smart lock systems are not just two ways to unlock a door. They are two different operating models. A cloud system buys vendor-managed software, remote access, integrations, and support. A local system buys more control, lower recurring fees, and more responsibility for the network, backups, documentation, and security.

The mistake is comparing only the first hardware quote. A cloud proposal may look clean because the monthly fee hides support, remote access, and integrations in one line. A local deployment may look cheaper because there is no per-door subscription, but the labor shifts to whoever maintains the hubs, servers, VPN, automations, backups, and access policies. The real comparison is total cost of ownership, not sticker price.

Use the [Subscription vs Purchase Calculator](/calculators/subscription-compare) and [Installation Cost Estimator](/calculators/installation-cost) before choosing either path. The answer changes quickly by door count, user count, turnover rate, integration needs, and the cost of a lockout.

## What Cloud Systems Really Buy

Cloud systems are attractive because they reduce the amount of infrastructure the owner must run. For apartments, gyms, coworking spaces, short-term rentals, and offices, that can be valuable. The vendor may handle remote access, app updates, user management, PMS or booking integrations, audit logs, role permissions, and support escalation.

That convenience has a recurring cost. Per-door pricing is common, but it is not the only model. Some vendors price by active user, some by building, and some bundle hardware into a higher monthly fee. Per-user pricing is especially risky for high-turnover or high-membership businesses because the access cost grows with credential count rather than door count.

The other cloud cost is exit risk. Before signing, ask what happens if the subscription ends, the vendor is acquired, or the service shuts down. In a soft-cloud design, the keypad and local programming may still work even if remote management stops. In a hard-cloud design, losing the service can damage the entire access workflow. That question belongs in procurement, not after cancellation.

## What Local Systems Really Cost

Local systems reduce vendor rent, but they do not remove operating cost. Someone still has to keep the controller healthy, document the topology, manage backups, update firmware, secure remote access, monitor batteries, and recover the system after hardware failure. In a small home or technical owner-operator setup, that may be acceptable. In a managed property, it is labor that needs a named owner.

Local also changes integration work. Connecting a self-hosted Z-Wave or Zigbee system to booking software, staff directories, or property-management workflows may require middleware or custom automation. If one technical employee built that workflow and nobody else understands it, the system has a continuity risk even if the monthly bill is low.

Security responsibility also shifts. A cloud vendor is not automatically safer, but it usually owns patching and remote-access infrastructure. With a local system, the property owner must avoid exposed admin panels, weak VPN setups, undocumented passwords, and unpatched controller software.

## Hardware Refresh and Battery Labor

Both architectures need realistic hardware assumptions. Smart locks are electronics mounted on moving doors, exposed to vibration, weather, tenant behavior, and battery limits. Mechanical hardware may last longer than the connected electronics, and a ten-year plan should expect some refresh or replacement.

Battery labor can be just as important as subscription cost. Wi-Fi locks may be easier to deploy but can create more battery visits, especially on weak signals. Low-power mesh locks may reduce battery work but require hub and repeater planning. The cheaper architecture on paper is not cheaper if it creates emergency truck rolls.

## How to Choose

Choose cloud when integrations, support accountability, staff turnover, and remote administration matter more than subscription avoidance. This is often true for hospitality, multifamily operators, gyms, and teams that do not have technical staff to maintain local infrastructure.

Choose local when data control, offline resilience, low recurring cost, and hardware ownership matter more than vendor-managed convenience. This can fit schools, technically capable facilities teams, private estates, and long-term owner-operators who are willing to document and maintain the system.

The best decision is usually not pure cloud or pure local. Many properties need local credential behavior at the door, cloud administration for staff, and a written exit plan if the vendor relationship changes. Put the cancellation scenario, hardware refresh plan, and maintenance owner in writing before deployment.
