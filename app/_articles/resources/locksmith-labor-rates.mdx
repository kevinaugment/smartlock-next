Smart lock installation pricing varies because the job is rarely just "swap the old deadbolt." The locksmith may be pricing travel time, door alignment, fresh drilling, strike-plate work, app setup, fire-door limitations, commercial hardware, and the risk of owning the result if the lock fails.

For a straightforward residential replacement on a prepared door, the labor may be modest. For a fresh bore, thick door, steel frame, mortise lock, storefront, or fire-rated opening, the price can rise quickly. Use the [Installation Cost Estimator](/calculators/installation-cost) before comparing quotes, because a cheap quote that excludes the hard part is not actually cheap.

## What You Are Paying For

A service call covers the cost of sending a technician, truck, tools, and insurance to the property. In dense cities, parking and travel time can matter as much as the lock itself. Retrofit labor covers removing the old lock, fitting the new hardware, aligning the latch, and making sure the motor can throw the deadbolt without strain.

Fresh installation is more expensive because the installer is cutting the door and frame. A new deadbolt bore has to be placed accurately, the latch face has to sit correctly, and the strike needs enough depth for the bolt. A crooked hole or shallow strike can leave the lock unreliable even if the app pairing works.

Door adjustment is not an upsell when the deadbolt drags. Smart locks have small motors. If the bolt does not move smoothly by hand, the motor will waste batteries, stall, or report jammed status. A good installer checks hinges, weatherstripping, latch depth, and strike alignment before blaming the electronics.

App and Wi-Fi setup may be priced separately. Some locksmiths install only the hardware and leave account creation to the owner. Others will pair the lock, update firmware, test codes, and confirm remote access. Clarify this before the appointment, especially for rentals where the owner is not on site.

## Residential Versus Commercial Work

Residential jobs are usually about fit, alignment, and owner setup. Commercial jobs add heavier hardware, steel frames, fire-rated doors, egress rules, low-voltage work, access-control commissioning, and documentation. That is why a common-area door, storefront, office suite, or multifamily corridor can cost much more than a single-family front door.

Commercial work should be scoped by door type, not by lock brand. A Grade 1 cylindrical lock, mortise lock, electric strike, panic bar, or card-reader integration all change the labor. Fire-rated openings deserve special caution: drilling or modifying the wrong part of a labeled door or frame can create compliance problems beyond the locksmith invoice. Review [Fire Rating Codes](/articles/resources/fire-rating-ul-codes) before approving field modification.

## Quote Red Flags

The most common pricing trap is a low service-call number that does not include meaningful labor. Ask whether the trip charge is included in the total estimate, whether fresh drilling is included, whether the technician will adjust the strike plate, and whether app setup or code testing is part of the job.

For commercial doors, ask whether the technician works on steel frames, mortise hardware, panic hardware, and low-voltage access control. A residential locksmith may be excellent at home deadbolts and still be the wrong person for a storefront or rated corridor door.

Also ask about warranty boundaries. If you buy the lock online and hire a locksmith only for installation, the locksmith may warranty the installation but not the electronics. If the motor fails, the manufacturer may own that issue. If the lock fails because the door was misaligned, the installer may own it. That boundary should be clear before work starts.

## DIY or Professional

DIY can make sense for one prepared residential door when the owner is comfortable with hand tools and app setup. Professional installation is usually worth it for fresh drilling, multiple doors, rental properties, commercial hardware, fire-rated doors, mortise locks, and any door where a bad cut would be expensive.

After installation, factory reset or claim the lock under the owner account, remove installer admin access if it was used, create a test code, and verify manual key operation. A finished smart lock job is not finished until the deadbolt moves smoothly, the backup entry method works, and the owner controls the admin account.
