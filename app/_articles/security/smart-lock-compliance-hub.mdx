## Compliance Is a Stack, Not One Checkbox

Smart lock compliance depends on where the lock is used and what data it collects. A vacation rental door raises different questions from a private home, a multifamily common entrance, an office, a hotel room, or a fire-rated stair door. The same lock can be a simple convenience in one setting and a regulated access-control device in another.

Think in layers: privacy for access logs and biometrics, accessibility for operable hardware, fire and egress rules for rated doors, local rental or short-term rental rules for guest access, insurance requirements for backup entry, and audit practices for incidents.

## Start With Data and Door Type

List what the system records: names, phone numbers, PIN assignments, access times, device IDs, video, biometrics, location signals, and admin actions. Then classify the door: private residential, rental unit, common area, commercial space, hotel, fire-rated opening, or regulated facility.

That combination determines how careful the process needs to be. A fingerprint lock in an owner-occupied home is not the same risk as biometric access for employees or guests. A keypad on a fire-rated door may be fine only if the listing, latch, closer, and egress behavior remain compliant.

## Keep Access Defensible

Use unique credentials instead of shared permanent codes. Define when access starts, when it ends, who can approve entry, and how logs are retained. For rentals and hospitality, keep guest access separate from owner or staff access. For workplaces, align lock access with onboarding and offboarding so physical access ends when employment or vendor work ends.

Audit logs should support security, operations, and incident review. They should not become casual surveillance. Tell staff, tenants, or guests what is collected when the context requires it, and keep retention rules clear before a dispute or claim happens.

## Do Not Break Egress or Fire Protection

Smart hardware should not prevent people from leaving, defeat listed fire hardware, or remove required emergency access. Fire-rated doors, stair doors, commercial doors, and accessibility-sensitive doors deserve professional review before hardware is changed.

The [Security Compliance Checker](/calculators/security-compliance), [Privacy Compliance Checker](/calculators/privacy-compliance), and [Fire Compliance Checker](/calculators/fire-compliance) can help frame the review, but local code, lease terms, platform rules, and insurer requirements still control the final answer.

## Common Mistakes

The recurring mistakes are predictable: treating guest-code convenience as permission to operate an unlawful short-term rental, installing hardware on rated doors without checking the listing, collecting biometric data without consent or an alternative access method, using logs for broad monitoring instead of defined events, and removing physical or emergency fallback paths.

If the door affects life safety, shared housing, employees, guests, insurance claims, or regulated operations, document the decision and involve the right professional before deployment.
