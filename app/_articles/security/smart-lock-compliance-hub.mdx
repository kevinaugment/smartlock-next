## Quick Answer

Smart lock compliance is not one rule. It is a stack: privacy law for access logs and biometrics, ADA accessibility for operable hardware, fire and egress rules for rated doors, local short-term rental rules for guest access, and insurance requirements for backup entry and claims evidence.

Use the [Security Compliance Checker](/calculators/security-compliance), [Privacy Compliance Checker](/calculators/privacy-compliance), and [Fire Compliance Checker](/calculators/fire-compliance) before deploying locks in rentals, multifamily buildings, hotels, offices, or regulated facilities.

## Deployment Checklist

1. Classify what data the lock collects: names, codes, access times, biometrics, device IDs.
2. Confirm whether the door is residential, multifamily, common-area, commercial, hotel, or fire-rated.
3. Keep emergency egress and backup entry intact.
4. Use unique credentials instead of shared permanent codes.
5. Define access-log retention before an incident occurs.
6. Confirm local STR, rental, employment, and accessibility rules where relevant.

## Common Compliance Mistakes

- Treating guest-code convenience as permission to operate an unlawful STR.
- Installing smart hardware on fire-rated doors without verifying listings and local requirements.
- Collecting biometric data without consent or an alternative access method.
- Using audit logs for routine surveillance rather than security and operational events.
- Removing physical or emergency access paths.
