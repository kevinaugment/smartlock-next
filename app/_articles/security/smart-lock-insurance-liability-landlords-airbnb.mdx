## Quick Answer

Smart locks can reduce insurance and liability risk only when access is controlled, documented, and backed up. Reused codes, dead batteries, missing emergency access, and unclear guest instructions can turn the lock into evidence of negligence instead of evidence of good management.

Use [Security Compliance Checker](/calculators/security-compliance), [Emergency Backup Planner](/calculators/emergency-backup), and [Audit Trail Guide](/articles/security/audit-trail-forensic-analysis) to build a defensible workflow.

## Liability Risk Map

| Scenario | Risk | Better control |
|---|---|---|
| Guest code reused | Unauthorized access claim | Unique code per stay |
| Battery dies during stay | Lockout, refund, emergency labor | Battery replacement threshold and backup key |
| Cleaner uses guest code | Audit confusion | Separate cleaner credential |
| Host enters without notice | Privacy or lease dispute | Written access policy and local law compliance |
| Lock log missing | Weak claim evidence | Retention policy and export process |

## Insurance Questions to Ask

- Does the policy require specific lock grades or deadbolt standards?
- Are short-term rental stays covered under the current policy?
- Are access logs useful or required during theft/damage claims?
- Does the insurer require physical key backup or local emergency access?
- Are common-area or multifamily doors subject to different requirements?

## Airbnb and Landlord Operating Rules

- Use time-limited codes for guests and vendors.
- Keep owner/admin codes out of guest messages.
- Store access logs only for documented security, dispute, or compliance reasons.
- Keep a backup access method that a local manager can use.
- Document battery replacement and lock testing.
- Remove users after checkout, move-out, employment change, or vendor completion.

## Evidence Checklist After an Incident

1. Export relevant access logs.
2. Preserve code assignment history.
3. Record battery status and lock health.
4. Document who had active credentials.
5. Save guest, tenant, cleaner, or vendor communications.
6. Avoid editing or deleting logs until the claim is resolved.
