## Quick Answer

California vacation rental smart lock compliance is local-first: city and county STR rules decide whether the rental is allowed, while privacy, fire safety, and access practices decide whether the lock workflow is defensible.

Use [Fire Compliance Checker](/calculators/fire-compliance), [Privacy Compliance Checker](/calculators/privacy-compliance), and [STR ROI Calculator](/calculators/str-roi) to connect legal risk, safety, and operating cost.

## California Smart Lock Compliance Checklist

| Area | What to check |
|---|---|
| Local STR permit | City/county license, display rules, occupancy limits |
| Guest access | Unique code per stay with expiration after checkout |
| Privacy | Notice for access logs and app data where appropriate |
| Fire safety | Fire-rated doors, egress, local inspection requirements |
| Emergency access | Physical key, local manager, or approved emergency procedure |
| Vendor access | Cleaner and maintenance codes separated from guest codes |

## Guest Code Policy

- Use one code per reservation.
- Do not reuse codes between unrelated guests.
- Start codes shortly before check-in.
- Expire codes after checkout plus a documented grace period.
- Keep cleaner, owner, and emergency codes separate.
- Review logs after incidents rather than as continuous guest monitoring.

For operational code setup, use [How to Create Temporary Guest Code](/articles/guides/create-temporary-guest-code).

## Fire and Door Hardware Concerns

Do not modify fire-rated doors or common-area egress hardware without confirming local requirements. A smart deadbolt that is acceptable on a single-family exterior door may be inappropriate on a rated corridor door or multifamily unit entry.

Use [Fire Ratings & Smart Locks](/articles/resources/fire-rating-guide), [UL 10C Guide](/articles/resources/fire-rating-ul-codes), and [ADA Compliance Guide](/articles/resources/ada-compliance-guide) for the standards layer.
